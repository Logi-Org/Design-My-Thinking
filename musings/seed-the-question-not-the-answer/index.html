Seed the question, not the answer.

How do you get a workshop moving without steering people towards your answer?

A blank page can slow a room down. A carefully chosen question can give people a useful place to start without telling them where they should finish.

By Daniel Logan-Gill · Workshop design and facilitation

The thought

Facilitators sometimes seed content because they are worried the group will stall. The safer move is to seed the inquiry instead: give participants a strong question that opens territory while leaving the answer genuinely theirs.

Why it helps

  • Reduces the intimidation of a completely blank start.
  • Helps quieter participants enter the conversation.
  • Expands the territory without smuggling in a preferred conclusion.

Try it in the room

Prepare three to five provocative but neutral questions. Put them on the wall or canvas before the discussion starts. Invite people to ignore them, rewrite them or add better questions if the room sees something you did not.

Watch for

A seeded question should not contain the facilitator's answer in disguise. If there is only one respectable way to respond, it is probably a leading question rather than a useful seed.

A good seeded question creates movement. A bad one quietly tells the room what to think.
