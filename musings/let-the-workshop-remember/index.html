Musing 06 · Workshop memory

Let the workshop remember.

How do you preserve the thinking between workshop activities, rather than just the final outputs?

Most workshop outputs capture where the room landed. In a recent face-to-face session, selective recording and fast AI summaries created a visible memory of how the thinking developed.

By Daniel Logan-Gill · Workshop design and facilitation

The thought

A lot of useful workshop thinking disappears almost as quickly as it happens. We remember the Post-its and the final decisions, but the thread connecting one conversation to the next is harder to hold onto.

With the group's permission, we selectively recorded plenary conversations, activity instructions and participant sharebacks. We switched recording off for breakout discussions. After each recorded section, we used the transcript to create a short AI summary, printed it, and added it to the wall.

Over the day, the wall became a visible memory of the workshop — not a verbatim transcript, but a trace of how the thinking developed.

Why it helps

  • Gives participants a quick way to revisit earlier conversations.
  • Makes the thread between activities and decisions easier to see.
  • Helps later reflection build on what the room has already discussed.
  • Gives sponsors a clearer narrative of how the group moved from the opening conversation to the eventual outcomes and next steps.

Try it in the room

  • Be selective. Record plenary conversations, activity briefs and sharebacks — not everything.
  • Switch off for breakouts. Those conversations are simultaneous, messy and difficult to capture cleanly.
  • Distil while it is fresh. After each recording, generate the transcript and ask AI for the main themes and useful points.
  • Keep it short. The summary needs to be scannable, not another transcript.
  • Print it and add it to the wall. Let the workshop's memory build while the workshop is still happening.

Record selectively → distil quickly → make the thinking visible.

Watch for

  • Consent first. Be explicit about what is being recorded, why and how it will be used.
  • AI summaries are interpretations. They can miss nuance or flatten disagreement, so treat them as a memory aid rather than the authoritative record.
  • More recording is not necessarily better. Capture the moments that help the room remember.
  • Protect the human conversation. The recording should support the workshop, never become the reason people hold back.
Most workshop artefacts show where we finished. There is real value in leaving behind a trace of how we got there.
