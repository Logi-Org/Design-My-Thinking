Prototype 03 · LinkedIn Conversation Engine

Find the conversations worth entering.

A small experiment in making LinkedIn more deliberate. Rather than starting with “what should I post?”, the engine starts with your themes, finds conversations where you may genuinely have something to add, helps you form a point of view, then drafts in a voice you can edit.

Try the public demo ↓Private workspace ↗

The idea in four moves.

1Sense: scan conversations against themes that matter to you.
2Decide: rank where there is a genuine contribution to make.
3Think: agree + extend, challenge, or reframe the conversation.
4Draft: turn the point of view into editable language. You still decide what gets posted.

What I was testing

Most AI writing tools make it easier to produce more content. I was more interested in whether a tool could help someone contribute less often, but with more relevance and a clearer point of view.

Signal

What deserves attention?

Use themes, freshness, conversation quality and contribution potential to filter down to a small number of worthwhile opportunities.

Thinking

What do I actually think?

Force a choice between extending, challenging or reframing before asking the model to write. Drafting comes after perspective.

Voice

Does it sound like a person?

Use source writing and editing history to learn patterns, while keeping every output editable and every LinkedIn action human-approved.

    Voice

    Draft like you, not like “LinkedIn”.

    The live workspace can learn from source writing and edits. This sample shows the kind of guidance the drafting layer can use.

    Voice profile
    Plain-spoken

    Prefer clear language over performance.

    Short explanations, ordinary words and no manufactured certainty.

    Practical

    Move from idea to application.

    Use an example, a question or a simple mental model people can actually pick up.

    Reflective

    Make room for nuance.

    It is fine to say where something is still unresolved rather than forcing a polished conclusion.

    Low-hype

    Avoid generic AI enthusiasm.

    Make the specific point. Skip phrases that could have been written under almost any post.

    Generic draft

    “Great insights. AI is transforming the way we work and organisations need to embrace the change.”

    Closer to the target voice

    “The part I keep coming back to is the hand-off between tasks. A better model does not help much if the surrounding decision still has no clear owner.”

    The live version stays private.

    Your workspace can use live discovery, your own themes and Voice DNA, LinkedIn notification signals and paid AI models. Authentication keeps that away from public visitors and protects API spend.
